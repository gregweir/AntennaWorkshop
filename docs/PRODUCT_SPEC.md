# Product Specification

## Product vision

RadioBook Antenna Workshop will be an approachable, comprehensive workbench for people who build their own antennas. It should help a newcomer succeed without concealing the assumptions, controls, and source material an experienced builder needs.

The application will run in a desktop or mobile browser, install as an offline-capable PWA, and ultimately be hosted below `radiobook.ca/antennas`.

## Confirmed decisions

- Canada is the default jurisdiction, with a prominent persistent selector.
- Canada, United States, and United Kingdom assessment workflows are available. Australia includes an ACMA class-licence assessment gateway, the official simple exclusion-distance method above 30 MHz, and the ARPANSA reference-table workflow; pattern-specific and near-field assessment methods require further scoping.
- Station location, not browsing location, determines the applicable jurisdiction. Network-derived location may suggest a first-visit default but never overrides an explicit selection.
- Metric is the default unit system. Imperial conversion is available everywhere through a single toggle.
- Design frequency inputs include jurisdiction-aware amateur-band selectors. The interface distinguishes conventional band names from the actual wavelength at the selected frequency.
- The standard experience is beginner-friendly; Advanced controls remain available from day one.
- The visual system follows RadioBook and Tartanleaf branding.
- The application remains useful offline and does not require an account.
- Source code is published under MPL-2.0. Tartanleaf and RadioBook brand identifiers remain separately protected.

## Current implementation

The public repository currently includes:

- Graphical antenna starting designs covering single- and multiband wire, vertical, loop, folded, directional, broadband, and receive-only families.
- Canada-, United States-, United Kingdom-, and Australia-aware amateur-band selection with metric and imperial presentation.
- Feedline loss, mismatch, coaxial stub, ERP, and EIRP tools.
- Official Canadian and United States RF power-density reference levels.
- A United States single-source FCC routine-evaluation exemption workflow covering all three criteria in 47 CFR § 1.1307(b)(3)(i), with ERP/EIRP distinctions and explicit non-compliance vocabulary.
- A United States multiple-source FCC exemption workflow covering the special 1 mW conditions and mixed close-proximity, distance-ERP, and existing-evaluation fractions in § 1.1307(b)(3)(ii).
- United Kingdom Ofcom workflows covering the no-action, retained average-power record, and assessment-required branches plus conservative v1.1 single- and multiple-transmitter compliance-distance reproductions with explicit antenna and reflection limitations.
- An Australian ACMA class-licence gateway for fixed and mobile low-risk categories, higher-risk assessment and record requirements, simultaneous frequencies, and shared sites; a versioned on-device clauses 4–6 record worksheet with current and transitional route selection, reproducibility prompts, structured station-change review, print, validated JSON import/export, and non-destructive saved revision history; and ARPANSA RPS S-1 reference levels.
- Conservative single-source far-field screening, frequency-normalized multiple-source aggregation, and printable multi-point station projects with independent source coordinates, accessible drag/touch/keyboard layout, illustrative azimuth envelopes, and descriptive source-to-point bearing analysis.
- An on-device station-project library with validated, versioned JSON import/export, a searchable and filterable evidence log scoped to projects, sources, points, and individual inputs, reusable templates, self-declared quality prompts, documentation summaries, and optional author-set review reminders. A derived assessment checklist keeps blockers, category and field-level documentation gaps, and external review distinct, and a guided action prepares the evidence form for a selected missing critical value. An optional station-assessment guide and contextual help explain shared coordinates, duty cycle, gain, antenna dimensions, height references, and inventory confirmation without hiding the complete controls. The workspace also supports non-destructive baseline-versus-scenario comparison, a typed audit of changed inputs, and read-only sensitivity curves with custom ranges, screening-trigger annotations, accessible data tables, and CSV export.
- A connected antenna-to-system-to-exposure workflow that preserves typed inputs and calculator-model provenance.
- An installable PWA shell, persistent preferences, explicit update prompts, and automated unit and browser tests.

Australian beta scope deliberately stops when the official simple method is inapplicable. Pattern-specific attenuation, near-field calculation, terrain and building modelling, uncertainty analysis, and measurement interpretation are not beta features; the application directs those cases to an applicable standards-based assessment or measurement. Independent RF review and production deployment remain release blockers.

## Core experience

Users can begin either by choosing an antenna family or by describing a goal such as portable HF, a fixed VHF antenna, or an RF exposure check. Each calculator provides:

1. Clear inputs with units, defaults, and contextual help.
2. A live, dimensioned construction diagram.
3. A cut list and practical build notes.
4. Formula, source, assumptions, applicability, and intermediate values.
5. A tuning procedure that distinguishes the calculated starting point from the finished antenna.
6. Saved projects, comparison, print, and export capabilities.

Contextual help must work with touch, keyboard, and pointer input. Complex sections may also provide an optional, collapsible step-by-step guide. Guidance should explain the existing workflow rather than create a separate simplified mode or conceal advanced controls.

The antenna workspace must provide an optional comparison of three user-selected construction planners. It must cover site footprint, band intent, feed and tuning system, directional context, build complexity, and required installed verification without assigning a performance ranking or inferring gain, efficiency, signal strength, or suitability at the user’s site.

The EFHW guide must distinguish impedance ratio from winding turns ratio, treat end impedance and multiband resonances as installation-dependent assumptions, and lead the builder through return-path planning, documented transformer selection, build-long construction, installed measurement, and per-band verification. It must not imply that the calculator designs or power-rates a transformer or prescribes one universal counterpoise.

The OCFD guide must explain how the selected split changes both leg lengths and likely feedpoint impedance, distinguish a nominal current-balun ratio from a complete balun and choking design, and preserve the selected split while trimming. It must require installed measurement on each intended band and avoid promising a match, pattern, or tuner-correctable common-mode system.

The fan-dipole guide must calculate each equal wire pair from its own design frequency, present total material and the longest span, and keep all pairs on one balanced feedpoint. It must require stable non-conductive spacing, symmetrical construction, highest-frequency-first tuning, and a complete band sweep after every adjustment. It must not combine ideal element impedances, predict mutual coupling, guarantee a tuner-free match, or claim a finished bandwidth or pattern.

The multiband-doublet guide must size two equal radiator legs from the lowest intended frequency and treat the radiator, balanced feedline, tuner interface, and short coax transition as one system. It may calculate physical and electrical line length and the documented eighth-wavelength adjustment increment, but must not infer complex load impedance, line SWR, mismatch loss, tuner range, balun stress, efficiency, bandwidth, or pattern. Guidance must require line clearance and balance, low-power commissioning on every band, and verification of tuner and balun heating at the intended power.

The single-band loaded-dipole and loaded-vertical guides may calculate the ideal series reactance and inductance that replace omitted radiator length using the documented single-wire transmission-line method. They must expose physical length, conductor diameter, correction factor, and loading position; the dipole must also expose model height above ground. A loaded dipole uses two equal coils, while a loaded vertical supports base or elevated loading and requires an explicit RF return-system plan. Results must be described as starting targets and must not infer physical turns, coil Q, self-capacitance, loss, heating, voltage, current, impedance, matching, bandwidth, efficiency, or pattern. Guidance must require measurement of the installed antenna and verification at intended power.

The two-band trap-dipole guide may solve the documented ideal parallel-LC network that supplies inductive reactance at the lower design frequency and capacitive reactance at the upper design frequency for a selected span, height, conductor diameter, and symmetric trap position. It must enforce the method's wire-section constraints, show that the trap resonance lies between the operating frequencies, and require two measured, matched traps. It must not infer component technology, power rating, Q, parasitics, tolerance, loss, heating, voltage, feedpoint impedance, matching, bandwidth, efficiency, or pattern. Both operating bands must be rechecked after every installed adjustment.

The physical coil planner may translate an antenna-derived inductance target into a single-layer air-core coil using the documented ARRL approximation. It must use form outside diameter, insulated-wire outside diameter, and winding length to calculate mean diameter, ideal and whole-turn estimates, pitch, spacing, and approximate winding-wire length. It must enforce the published minimum winding-length-to-diameter ratio and identify when the selected wire cannot fit in one layer. It must not infer RF Q, resistance, self-capacitance, self-resonance, current, voltage, heating, corona, form loss, environmental stability, or a finished measured inductance.

The trap commissioning worksheet must record two completed traps independently, distinguish LC-calculated resonance from instrument-measured resonance, show deviation from the design targets, and calculate pair spread relative to the pair average. User-selected L and C target windows must remain separate from the pair-matching threshold. It must support identifiable on-device snapshots, a print layout that expands multiline notes, and versioned validated JSON import and export without treating browser storage as archival. Imported records receive new local identities. Threshold labels may describe construction consistency only and must not imply RF performance, measurement certainty, Q, bandwidth, loss, current or voltage capability, heating, weather resistance, antenna efficiency, or safe operating power. Guidance must preserve fixture, calibration, lead, frequency, and environmental conditions and require installed low-power verification on both bands.

The small-transmitting-loop guide must size a circular loop from entered circumference, apply the ARRL strict under-one-sixteenth-wavelength diameter boundary at the highest requested frequency, and calculate ideal resonating capacitance from a measured finished-loop inductance. It must keep total capacitance, separately entered equivalent parallel fixed or stray capacitance, and the required variable-capacitor range distinct and report whether the entered usable capacitor range covers both frequency edges. It must not imply that distributed loop capacitance can always be represented as one lumped parallel value. The feed-coupling drawing is conceptual and must not prescribe a coupling-loop size. The model must not estimate inductance from ideal geometry or infer radiation resistance, loss resistance, efficiency, Q, bandwidth, current, voltage, capacitor power rating, matching, pattern, exposure, or safe operating power.

The phased-wire-collinear guide must keep corrected half-wave radiator sections and velocity-factor-adjusted quarter-wave phase-reversing stubs distinct. It supports two to eight equal radiator sections, requires one identical shorted two-conductor stub between every adjacent pair, keeps radiator and stub trim allowances separate, and totals the radiator and both conductors of every stub. It may identify the sourced preference for balanced current feed with odd section counts and balanced voltage feed with even counts, plus clearly labelled historical impedance context; neither is an installed impedance prediction or matching design. Its diagram must show construction sequence without prescribing a feedpoint or matching system. The planner must require measured installed tuning and must not infer impedance, gain, beamwidth, sidelobes, bandwidth, loss, efficiency, current, voltage, common-mode behaviour, exposure, or safe operating power.

The two-element cubical-quad guide must scale the sourced driven-loop perimeter, preserve the reflector at five percent larger, calculate equal diamond sides and centre-to-corner spreader arms, and convert an explicit 0.10–0.25-wavelength element-spacing input into boom spacing. The feedpoint marker may explain bottom-corner horizontal polarization and side-corner vertical polarization without implying a completed feed or matching system. Trim allowance applies to both loops while preserving their ratio. The planner must require electromagnetic modelling and installed verification and must not infer impedance, matching, gain, beamwidth, front-to-back ratio, sidelobes, bandwidth, structural loading, current, voltage, exposure, or safe operating power.

The axial-mode helix guide must calculate circumference, diameter, turn spacing, axial length, conductor path, and ground-plane diameter from explicit wavelength ratios, pitch angle, and whole-number turn count. It may show classical sourced estimates for beamwidth, gain, terminal resistance, and on-axis axial ratio only when clearly separated from construction dimensions and labelled as estimates requiring modelling and measurement. Direct-endfire winding handedness may identify RHCP or LHCP and must warn that a dish reflection reverses polarization sense. The workflow must not imply a completed 50 Ω feed, verified pattern, bandwidth, polarization purity, exposure assessment, or safe power rating.

The discone guide must use the sourced lower-frequency cone-slant and disc-diameter equations, accept a 25–40° cone half-angle, and derive cone height and base diameter geometrically. Radial disc and cone constructions must expose whole-number element counts, individual cut lengths, and total conductor while explaining that radial structures only approximate solid surfaces. The feed guidance must keep the disc-to-cone separation as short as practical without inventing a universal gap dimension. The workflow must not infer an upper-frequency limit, bandwidth, impedance, VSWR, gain, elevation pattern, exposure result, or safe power rating.

The log-periodic dipole-array guide must implement Carrel’s τ element scaling, σ centre-spacing definition, apex-angle relationship, and structural-bandwidth equation. It must distinguish requested operating bandwidth from structural bandwidth through an explicit active-region factor, round the element count up, and expose every full cut length, half length, next-element spacing, boom position, half-wave reference, and alternating balanced-boom connection. It must show the short-element feed end and forward direction while treating the boom line, transition, termination, conductor geometry, match, gain, pattern, measured bandwidth, exposure result, and safe power rating as separate modelling and verification work.

The Beverage guide must remain receive-only and calculate a straight horizontal route from reference frequency, wavelength multiple, average height, and true bearing. It must show plan and elevation views, wire cut allowance, endpoint survey offsets, stable ground-return context, and the expected direction convention for a terminated far end. Terminated and unterminated comparison modes must remain distinct. Antenna-side impedance, feedline impedance, termination resistance, and nominal transformer ratio must be explicit assumptions; the workflow must not infer soil-dependent surge impedance, gain, RDF, beamwidth, front-to-back ratio, pattern, receiver safety, exposure result, or transmit power handling. It must not offer the transmit-system handoff.

The compact directional receive guide must remain receive-only and provide distinct Flag, point-fed Pennant, and EWE construction topologies. Sourced presets may load physical dimensions, clearance, termination, and feedpoint-resistance context, but frequency must supply wavelength context rather than silently rescale a preset. The interface must state that primary response is toward the feed end and the principal null toward the termination, explicitly distinguishing this convention from a terminated Beverage. It must total conductor, show cut allowance, true bearings, nominal impedance and turns ratios, and require two ground returns only for the EWE. It must not infer optimum termination, installed impedance, gain, RDF, beamwidth, front-to-back ratio, null depth, pattern, transformer performance, receiver safety, exposure result, or transmit power handling, and it must not offer the transmit-system handoff.

The K9AY guide must remain receive-only and model two identical quasi-delta loops at right angles on one support and shared ground. It must derive four true receive bearings from one reference bearing and map each bearing to loop selection plus reference or reversed feed/termination state. The diagram must distinguish both loop axes, the selected feed-end direction, the opposite termination/null direction, the quasi-delta elevation, and the shared base circuit. Published geometry, wire, termination, and transformer values may be defaults only. It must total per-loop and two-loop conductor, warn when a loop reaches 0.30 wavelength, keep control-path design outside the calculation, and must not infer termination optimum, installed impedance, gain, RDF, beamwidth, front-to-back ratio, null depth, pattern, transformer or relay performance, receiver safety, exposure result, or transmit power handling. It must not offer the transmit-system handoff.

The Loop-on-Ground guide must remain receive-only and model a corner-fed square of insulated wire laid directly on the surface. Frequency supplies electrical-size context but must not silently rescale the physical loop. The planner must show perimeter, cut allowance, diagonal, area, broad bidirectional low-angle axis, perpendicular feedpoint corner, isolated antenna and coax transformer windings, and the absence of an antenna earth connection. It must warn when circumference reaches one wavelength and distinguish building-entry bonding from grounding the loop. A receive-family comparison may describe topology, site, direction, ground, and switching differences but must not rank performance. The workflow must not infer antenna impedance, signal-to-noise improvement, gain, RDF, beamwidth, pattern, polarization purity, soil loss, transformer performance, receiver safety, exposure result, or transmit power handling, and it must not offer the transmit-system handoff.

The untuned active receiving-loop guide must remain receive-only and model one circular vertical turn with a balanced amplifier at the bottom feed gap. Lower and upper receive edges must provide electrical-size context without silently rescaling the physical diameter. The planner must show circumference, cut allowance, area, base and top height, loop plane, and expected bidirectional null axis. It must independently warn when the upper-edge perimeter reaches 0.10 wavelength and when diameter reaches the ARRL one-sixteenth-wavelength small-loop boundary. Guidance must cover purpose-designed balanced amplification, strong-signal overload, common-mode isolation, DC power, weather protection, entry protection, receiver protection, and positive transmit isolation. It must not design an amplifier or infer impedance, gain, sensitivity, noise figure, dynamic range, overload margin, bandwidth, signal-to-noise improvement, RDF, beamwidth, null depth, pattern, receiver safety, exposure result, or transmit power handling, and it must not offer the transmit-system handoff.

The receive-antenna selection wizard must evaluate all implemented receive families from explicit site and system constraints: reference frequency, maximum horizontal clearance, maximum support height, protected surface-wire permission, allowable antenna-circuit ground returns, active-electronics acceptance, switching acceptance, and fixed, four-direction, or continuously rotatable direction control. It must preserve sourced starting geometry, explain every blocker and direction-control tradeoff, keep building-entry bonding distinct from antenna ground returns, and open the matching planner and compact geometry. Results must not rank or infer gain, signal-to-noise ratio, sensitivity, RDF, beamwidth, pattern, null depth, bandwidth, soil loss, amplifier performance, receiver safety, exposure result, or installed reception.

The primary antenna picker must offer Guided and Workbench modes without changing calculator results. Guided mode must ask one question at a time, distinguish transmit-capable from receive-only construction, narrow by plain-language site or operating goals, show progress, support backward navigation, and label final options as relevant planners rather than recommendations or performance rankings. Workbench mode must search names, descriptions, categories, and practical tags; filter by Wire, Vertical, Directional, Loop, Broadband, and Receive-only categories; report result counts; and provide a clear empty-state reset. Choosing a design in either mode must preserve access to its complete advanced controls, help, assumptions, and limitations. Result cards across the connected antenna, feedline, and regulatory workflows must identify whether each value is calculated, a published starting point, an entered assumption, or a quantity that still requires finished-build measurement. Essential antenna construction dimensions and measurement warnings remain visible; secondary inputs, references, and supporting calculations may use a labelled disclosure. Regulatory status language remains separate from this evidence basis and must not be reduced to a generic pass or fail.

J-pole and Slim Jim guides must explain the construction-medium correction factor, parallel matching geometry, paired coax-tap adjustment, feedline choking, and installed vertical measurement. Slim Jim guidance must additionally preserve the interrupted folded side and gap. Neither guide may promise feedpoint impedance, bandwidth, pattern, or a universal tap or gap position from a low-SWR result.

Quarter-wave vertical guidance must distinguish a ground-mounted RF radial field from equal elevated counterpoise radials. The ground-mounted workflow must cover radial count and length, the radiator-only trim allowance, base and feed connections, and separate lightning and electrical-safety grounding. The elevated ground-plane workflow must cover equal radial count, symmetrical slope, shared cut allowance, coax routing, and common-mode control. Neither may promise impedance, efficiency, matching, or installed pattern.

Centre-fed dipole and inverted-V guidance must preserve equal legs, balanced feeding, common-mode control, endpoint safety, final support geometry, and symmetrical trimming. Inverted-V guidance must explain that the apex angle changes span and drop without prescribing support height. Full-wave-loop guidance must preserve total perimeter while distinguishing the selected idealized shape from the installed plane, feedpoint, matching, and distorted geometry. These guides may not promise impedance, bandwidth, multiband response, or pattern.

Directional-antenna guidance must preserve the Moxon rectangle's constant-diameter assumption, symmetrical A–E geometry, critical gaps, balanced feeding, and installed pattern verification. The basic Yagi guide must treat the element lengths, 0.0085λ diameter, centre-to-centre spacing, folded driven element, matching arrangement, and non-conductive boom as one NBS reference system. Material, boom, mounting, or feed substitutions require separate modelling and measurement; neither guide may promise SWR, gain, bandwidth, front-to-back ratio, or installed pattern.

Feedline guidance must distinguish manufacturer matched-loss data from installed loss under mismatch, use the complete cable and accessory path, identify the measurement reference plane, and explain that SWR does not reveal complex impedance. Coaxial-stub guidance must distinguish electrical from physical length, use the actual cable velocity factor, include connectors and termination geometry, and require calibrated measurement and trimming at the design frequency. Neither guide may imply a transmission-line simulation, power rating, complete matching-network design, or guaranteed bandwidth.

Equivalent-radiated-power guidance must preserve the transmitter-power reference plane and definition, document feed-system loss, use antenna gain rather than uncorrected directivity, and keep the selected dBi or dBd reference attached to the entered value. Exposure duty cycle and installed geometry remain separate station-project inputs and must not silently reduce the displayed ERP or EIRP. The workflow may not represent a directional equivalent power as total radiated power, pattern prediction, time averaging, near-field analysis, or compliance.

## Visual language

Visuals are functional rather than decorative:

- **Construction drawing:** dimensioned and intended for building.
- **Conceptual illustration:** educational and explicitly not to scale.
- **Calculated estimate:** derived from a documented formula.
- **Simulated result:** produced by an identified model and configuration.

Initial visuals cover a dipole or inverted-V, vertical with radials, loop, Moxon or Yagi, feedline path, tuning plot, and RF exposure side view. Colour reinforces meaning but never carries it alone.

## Initial calculator roadmap

The first useful release includes wavelength, dipole, fan dipole, balanced-line doublet, classic and half-size G5RV plus ZS6BKW family presets, inverted-V, quarter-wave vertical, ground plane, full-wave loop, EFHW, off-centre-fed dipole, J-pole, Slim Jim, Moxon, basic Yagi, feedline loss, velocity factor, coaxial stubs, SWR, return loss, mismatch loss, ERP/EIRP, and unit conversion.

Advanced families later include other multiband designs, detailed small-loop loss and component modelling, detailed collinear-array, cubical-quad, helical, discone, log-periodic, and Beverage modelling, additional receive antennas, detailed trap and capacitor construction, and matching networks.

## RF exposure

Exposure tools are jurisdiction-specific and versioned. The first release includes separate Canadian and United States workflows, multiple transmitters, controlled and uncontrolled environments, duty cycle, feedline loss, antenna gain, ground reflection, accessible locations, and printable records.

The result vocabulary must distinguish:

- a screen that demonstrates compliance under its stated conservative assumptions;
- a screen that is inconclusive and requires detailed calculation, measurement, or mitigation;
- an invalid scenario outside the method's applicability.

The application must not turn an inconclusive simplified screen into a claim of non-compliance or certification.

Exposure guidance must require the station jurisdiction, a defensible controlled or uncontrolled environment classification, the exact evaluation frequency, and the authority's averaging treatment. The single-source workflow must identify one accessible point, use supported power, duty-cycle, loss, gain, antenna-dimension, and three-dimensional separation inputs, stop at the conservative far-field boundary, and remain inconclusive until other relevant sources are assessed. Multi-source guidance must normalize each contribution against its own frequency-specific reference level at the same point before summing. A reference lookup or below-trigger result must never be described as safe, exempt, certified, or compliant.

The FCC single-source exemption workflow must evaluate the 1 mW, 0.5–40 cm `Pth`, and distance-based ERP criteria independently. It must use the FCC definitions of available maximum time-averaged power, ERP, and minimum body separation; enforce each frequency and distance boundary; and state that exemption is solely from routine environmental evaluation. Unknown-ERP substitution, SAR, mitigation, and compliance evaluation remain outside this model.

The FCC multiple-source workflow must keep the special 1 mW path separate from the fractional equation, enforce the strict total-below-1-mW and 2 cm alternatives, and require a supported contribution method for each source. It must stop when a selected method is inapplicable or the complete source set, fixed positional relationship, and common time-averaging basis are not confirmed. Existing evaluated exposures and limits must use the same quantity and units.

The Ofcom gateway must distinguish EIRP from ERP, use maximum radiated power in the strongest direction, and average the conservative maximum transmission time over six minutes. It must treat 10 W EIRP and 6.1 W ERP as inclusive no-action maximum-power thresholds. The recorded-average branch additionally requires no more than 100 W EIRP or 61 W ERP peak. It must identify when a calculation record is required and must not imply that the gateway calculates an exclusion distance or demonstrates compliance.

The Ofcom compliance-distance workflow must reproduce calculator v1.1 frequency boundaries, EIRP conversion, six-minute averaging, reflection coefficients, ICNIRP 1998 reference levels, and E/H treatment below 10 MHz. It must disclose when an antenna exceeds half a wavelength or a highly reflective surface can invalidate the simplified assumptions. A distance result must be measured from the closest part of the antenna, retained with its inputs and source revision, and never presented as compliance without the required public-access controls.

The conservative Ofcom multiple-transmitter workflow must convert each source to EIRP, sum all source powers, combine each source’s maximum transmission time within six minutes, and cap that combined input at the calculator’s 360-second maximum. It must calculate the summed power and combined time at every distinct entered frequency and select the greatest distance. Duplicate frequency entries may share one evaluation but must remain individually visible in the source inventory. The workflow must state that this method is deliberately very conservative and does not model separate antenna positions, simultaneous field addition, patterns, or equipment belonging to other spectrum users.

The Australian reference workflow must reproduce RPS S-1 (Rev. 1) Tables 4, 5, 7, and 8 from 100 kHz to 300 GHz. It must keep general-public and occupational scenarios, whole-body and local spatial bases, 30- and 6-minute averaging periods, electric field, magnetic field, incident power density, peak fields, and limb current distinct. “ES” must be explained as an electrostimulation-limited case rather than no restriction. The workflow must not offer the existing Canadian/United States far-field screen under Australian jurisdiction until its method and regulatory applicability are separately reviewed.

The Australian class-licence gateway must evaluate the three Schedule 1 clause 3 low-risk paths independently: fixed stations at no more than 100 W average total power with every antenna inaccessible to the general public; fixed stations with the lowest antenna base at least 10 m above ground and no more than 3200 W average total EIRP in any direction; and mobile stations at no more than 100 W average total power. When none applies, it must direct the user to the clause 4 measurement or calculation and clause 6 record package without implying non-compliance. Low-risk status must not be described as an exemption from the public-exposure condition. Simultaneous frequencies, shared sites, changed stations, transitional assessments, and agent agreements must remain visible as separate considerations.

The Australian simple exclusion-distance workflow must reproduce ACMA's published ARPANSA 2021 workbook above 30 MHz through 300 GHz. It uses transmitter output power without loss or duty-cycle reduction, converts dBd to dBi by adding 2.15 dB, applies maximum linear gain in every direction, and uses the general-public whole-body power-density bands of 2 W/m², frequency/200, and 10 W/m². The workbook equation uses `3.14` explicitly and must be reproduced without silently substituting another model. A separate applicability check must compare the result with the Amateur Class Licence far-field boundary `max(2D²/λ, 0.5λ)`. A result inside that boundary must direct the user to an applicable near-field method or measurement. The interface must disclose omitted environmental effects, losses, antenna pattern, multiple and simultaneous sources, uncertainty, terrain, buildings, and access-control validation.

The gateway may start a new unsaved record worksheet using the entered station type, canonical metric quantities, accessibility and shared-site selections, simultaneous-frequency selection, gateway branch, timestamp, and model identity. The transfer must not overwrite a saved record or infer an assessment route, standard, scope, method, assessor, measurement, calculation, conclusion, declaration, or compliance status. The receiving worksheet must identify which text is transferred input and which work remains to be documented.

The Australian record worksheet must capture the clause 6 responsible-person identity and declaration, station location and antenna details, power, gain, size, tilt, manufacturer, model, emission mode, assessor identity and qualification, assessment date, measurements or calculations, and any agent-agreement reference. Corporate-only and agent-only fields must be conditional. Draft records must remain on-device, use a versioned validated schema, support a dedicated archival print report and validated JSON import/export, and show missing named fields without scoring compliance or verifying their contents. The print report must reproduce complete multiline values, assessment evidence, declaration state, station-change entries, documentation summaries, source identity, and saved-versus-draft state without printing interactive form controls or claiming certification. Imports must receive new local identities instead of overwriting records with matching source IDs. Substantive saved edits must preserve restorable earlier revisions, and restoration must first produce an unsaved draft. The interface must warn that browser storage is not an archival system and that changed declaration information requires a new declaration.

The worksheet must distinguish a new clause 4 assessment under an applicable AS/NZS 2772.2, IEEE C95.3, or IEC 62232 edition from each transitional route in subclauses 4(4), 4(6), 4(7), and 4(8). It must separately prompt for the retained record, method and scope, highest normal operating conditions, public-access locations, equipment or model, calibration or validation, uncertainty, simultaneous-frequency treatment, results, conclusion, supporting references, and station-change review. These supporting prompts improve reproducibility but must not be labelled as statutory fields, used to calculate compliance, or treated as validation that a standard or transition applies.

The station-change log must identify the categories named in clause 5 as well as antenna-type, manufacturer/model, and responsible-person changes relevant to the retained record or declaration review. Each entry must preserve its effective date, description, post-change clause 3 review, clause 4 assessment action, clause 6 declaration action, reviewer, and supporting references. The workflow must not infer that every change invokes clause 4, that a previous assessment remains applicable, or that a new declaration is unnecessary. Entries remain part of the unsaved worksheet until the parent record is saved, at which point normal revision history applies.

## Acceptance criteria for the first public beta

- Responsive operation on current Android, iOS, and desktop browsers.
- Automated smoke coverage on Chromium, Firefox, and WebKit desktop engines plus representative Android and iPhone profiles, with physical Android and iOS installation checks required before public release.
- Keyboard and screen-reader operability, visible focus, and 44-pixel minimum targets.
- Cold offline return after one complete online visit.
- Persistent projects survive reload, browser restart, and application upgrade.
- Every shipped calculator has traceable sources and automated reference tests.
- Exposure calculations pass independent review and official-example cross-checks.
- Production output works correctly from the `/antennas/` subpath.
