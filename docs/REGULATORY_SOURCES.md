# Regulatory Source Registry

RF exposure methods must be implemented from primary sources, retain their revision identity, and be reviewed whenever a source changes. The FCC Table 1 MPE power-density limits and Safety Code 6 Tables 5–6 power-density reference levels are encoded and boundary-tested. A conservative single-source far-field estimate is available for education and preliminary screening. It uses average EIRP, a 2.56 reflected-field multiplier, and an explicit far-field applicability check. A separate tool sums multiple sources after normalizing every power density against the limit at its own frequency. Neither tool demonstrates compliance. The FCC single- and multiple-source routine-evaluation exemption criteria are implemented separately. Antenna-pattern attenuation, near-field evaluation, measurement uncertainty, and compliance classification remain disabled pending implementation and independent review.

The [RF exposure independent review package](RF_EXPOSURE_REVIEW.md) maps these methods to their code, equations, reproducible vectors, unresolved technical decisions, and review record.

## Canada

- ISED RBR-4 Schedule I — source for Canadian amateur allocation ranges displayed by band selectors.
- ISED CPC-2-0-03, *Radiocommunication and Broadcasting Antenna Systems* — establishes ongoing Safety Code 6 responsibilities and consideration of the local radio environment.
- Health Canada, *Safety Code 6* and its technical guide — controlled and uncontrolled reference levels and assessment guidance.
- ISED TN-261, *Safety Code 6 Radio Frequency Exposure Compliance Evaluation Template* — simplified screening, near/far-field treatment, multiple sources, and escalation criteria.
- ISED GL-01 and GL-08 — measurement and compliance-report guidance.

## Implementation rule

Every regulatory result must display its jurisdiction, source revision, last-reviewed date, scenario classification, assumptions, units, and limitations. Source updates require reference-vector review before release.

The FCC single-source exemption model implements all three independent paths in 47 CFR § 1.1307(b)(3)(i): available maximum time-averaged power no greater than 1 mW; the close-proximity `Pth` equation from 0.5 to 40 cm and 0.3 to 6 GHz; and the frequency-specific Table 1 ERP threshold at a separation of at least `λ/(2π)`. The close-proximity comparison uses the greater of available maximum time-averaged power or ERP. The distance method requires entered ERP and does not apply the rule's conditional substitution for unknown ERP. A qualifying result means only exemption from routine environmental evaluation; it does not waive compliance with § 1.1310 or other obligations.

The FCC multiple-source model implements § 1.1307(b)(3)(ii) as two separate alternatives. The special low-power path requires every source to be no more than 1 mW and either a total strictly below 1 mW or at least 2 cm between simultaneously operating radiating structures. The fractional path sums close-proximity `P/Pth`, distance-based `ERP/ERPth`, and supported existing `Evaluated/Exposure Limit` terms and qualifies at a total no greater than one. It requires confirmation of the complete source set, a fixed positional relationship, a common time-averaging basis, and an applicable selected method for every included source. It does not verify existing evaluations or discover omitted sources.

The Ofcom assessment gateway implements the decision thresholds in guidance paragraphs 6.10–6.19. Equipment at or below 10 W EIRP or 6.1 W ERP follows the no-assessment, no-record maximum-power branch. Above that level, average radiated power is maximum radiated power multiplied by the maximum transmission seconds in any six-minute period and divided by 360. No assessment is required when that average is at or below 10 W EIRP or 6.1 W ERP and peak power is at or below 100 W EIRP or 61 W ERP, but the calculation record must be retained. Exceeding either cap requires an assessment and record; it does not establish non-compliance.

The Ofcom compliance-distance model reproduces calculator v1.1. ERP is multiplied by 1.64 to obtain EIRP; average EIRP uses the maximum transmitting seconds in six minutes. The reflection coefficient is 1.0 through 12 MHz and 0.6 above 12 MHz. From 1 MHz to below 10 MHz, distance is the larger result obtained from the ICNIRP 1998 E- and H-field reference levels. From 10 MHz through 300 GHz, the model uses the calculator’s piecewise power-density reference level. The 450 MHz worked vector with 200 W EIRP and two transmitting minutes reproduces Ofcom’s published 2.46 m result. The tool flags antennas longer than half a wavelength and nearby highly reflective surfaces because Ofcom identifies these as limitations that may require more advanced assessment. It does not model complex installations, antenna patterns, multiple simultaneous transmitters, terrain, buildings, or access control, and a calculated distance is not by itself proof of compliance.

The preliminary far-field screen uses the maximum stated antenna gain and the TN-261 modified free-space expression, including the 2.56 power-density multiplier arising from a reflected electric field 60% as strong as the direct field. Canadian uncontrolled scenarios escalate for detailed review at 50% of the applicable Safety Code 6 limit. For all jurisdictions, the result is inconclusive when other RF sources have not been assessed and inapplicable inside the conservative far-field boundary.

Multiple-source aggregation implements the normalized sum `F = Σ(Pᵢ/Sᵢ)` described by ISED BPR-1 section 8.1. Every entered power density must describe the same evaluation point and use an averaging treatment appropriate to that source. The result remains inconclusive until the user confirms the relevant local source inventory has been assessed. The tool does not infer missing emitters or validate the method used to obtain an entered power density.

The station-project workflow applies the same single-source far-field calculation and normalized aggregation to several transmitters at several accessible locations. Straight-line separation is derived independently for every source–point path from east, north, and height coordinates. Any path inside its conservative far-field boundary makes that project location inapplicable to this method. The interactive plan is an input convenience: it synchronizes with the numeric coordinates but adds no survey, property-boundary, terrain, or mapping accuracy. Users may record an azimuth pattern category, maximum-response bearing relative to true north, and approximate horizontal beamwidth. For each source–point path, the tool derives plan bearing clockwise from true north, angular distance from the nearest entered lobe, and whether that bearing lies inside the entered illustrative envelope. Coincident plan coordinates have no horizontal bearing. These classifications are descriptive only; no pattern attenuation is applied and maximum entered gain remains directed toward every evaluation point. Plan and elevation diagrams expose the entered geometry, and the printable record preserves inputs, results, model identities, and limitations, but it is not a compliance certificate.

Station-project records are stored locally in the browser. Portable `.antenna-project.json` files use a versioned schema and are revalidated against the calculation model during import. Schema version 7 adds an optional author-selected evidence review reminder; version 6 entries migrate without a reminder. Schema version 6 added category-specific, self-declared record-quality checks; version 5 entries migrate with every quality prompt left unrecorded. Schema version 5 scopes each evidence entry to the project, a stable station-source or evaluation-point identity, and a related input; version 4 entries migrate to general project scope. Schema version 4 added the structured evidence log, version 3 records migrate to an empty log, and versions 1 and 2 also migrate to an explicitly unknown azimuth pattern. Evidence entries record a category, title, detailed note, review date, optional review reminder, reviewer, optional HTTP(S) reference, scope, and acknowledged quality prompts. Removing a scoped entity preserves its notes as general project evidence. Evidence improves traceability but does not establish the accuracy of an assumption or measurement. A reminder reports only that its author-selected date has arrived; it does not determine validity or expiry. Importing a file does not establish that its source inventory is complete or that its assumptions remain current.

The optional station-project guide and field help explain workflow order and input definitions, including the shared coordinate origin, duty cycle, maximum gain, largest antenna dimension, height datum, and source-inventory confirmation. This guidance does not select values, verify the station model, or expand the simplified method's applicability.

The assessment review checklist is derived from the current project and is not stored as an approval state. It separately reports calculation-input validity, source-inventory confirmation, far-field applicability, preliminary screening outcome, presence of each evidence category, field-specific evidence coverage for critical calculated values, and the need for external technical review. Its guided documentation action prepares a new evidence draft with the selected project, source, or point and related input; it never fills an evidence value or alters saved entries. Reusable templates can select an evidence category, suggest a title, and provide a detail prompt while preserving the chosen scope and any existing unsaved text. They do not provide a measured value, citation, conclusion, reviewer, or verification. Category-specific quality prompts let the author declare which expected elements the record addresses; unchecked prompts remain visible as documentation follow-up. These acknowledgements are not independently verified and do not affect field-specific evidence coverage. General notes do not count as field-specific coverage. Evidence checks confirm presence and scope only; they do not verify content, calibration, uncertainty, source authority, reviewer qualifications, or independence. The checklist deliberately provides no percentage or compliance score. Completing it is not a compliance determination or certification.

Scenario comparison duplicates a saved project before editing and matches sources, evaluation points, and evidence entries by their stable project identifiers. It reports the change in normalized fraction of the applicable reference level, expressed as percentage points, alongside a typed audit of project state, source settings, point geometry, and evidence. Added or removed entities are identified rather than inferred to be equivalent. Comparisons require the same jurisdiction and exposure environment. A lower scenario value is not proof of compliance and does not validate the changed assumptions.

Sensitivity analysis is read-only and recalculates the complete project while varying one selected input across a user-visible range. Transmitter output, duty cycle, and antenna-centre height affect the selected source only. Plan-distance analysis moves the selected point radially along its existing bearing from the selected source, so its distances from other sources may also change. Every sample retains the project’s far-field applicability checks and source-inventory state. The semantic table contains the plotted samples; its CSV export labels samples and interpolated crossings as separate record types, uses the active display unit, and repeats the screening-trigger fraction for review. Trigger crossings are linearly interpolated only between adjacent valid, far-field-applicable samples and remain approximate screening-model annotations. The curve is educational and is not an optimizer, exemption calculation, compliance limit, or compliance result.

The connected workflow transfers an antenna design’s frequency and largest physical dimension into the ERP/EIRP calculator, then combines them with explicitly entered transmitter power, feed-system loss, directional gain, duty cycle, installed antenna-centre height, and east/north site position. The station project preserves the contributing calculator model identities and resets source-inventory confirmation whenever a source is added. Transferred values still require review against the installed system.
